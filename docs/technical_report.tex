\title{Matched-Filter Recovery of Pasted Noise and a Twelve-Member Sound-Event Ensemble for Indic Speech Noise Event Detection}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Saifuddin}
\IEEEauthorblockA{Team syehsyoh; Senior Digital Transformation (AI \& Automation) Expert\\
Datathon@IndoML 2026, Track 1 (Noise Event Detection)\\
Code, heads and submissions: \url{https://github.com/syehsyoh-dokling/NoiseDetection}}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Track~1 asks for the onset and offset of every noise event in Vaani field recordings, scored by an event-based F1
(both boundaries within 20\,\% of the reference duration, at least 50\,ms) plus a per-clip segment Dice. Our final
submission (Codabench submission 994586) placed 5th of 82 teams with a combined score of 1.600895 (F1 0.780264,
Dice 0.820631; maximum 2.0). The synthetic clips appear to be clean speech with noise samples pasted from a finite
library; the validation release ships the clean references, so mixture minus reference recovers the pasted
waveforms, and a normalised cross-correlation (NCC) matched filter over 1,198 such templates locates them in the
test clips. Natural clips are handled by a frame-level sound-event detector (SED): a weighted mean of twelve
members, each a bidirectional GRU (BiGRU) head over a frozen or fine-tuned Whisper, AST, BEATs or Dasheng encoder,
decoded with per-event peak-relative boundaries. We report the leaderboard lineage, out-of-fold component scores,
and the reproduction of the submission after the original fine-tuned checkpoints were lost.
\end{abstract}

\section{Training Data and Preprocessing}
\textbf{Vaani data.} The released validation set holds 2,501 clips (3.51\,h, 16\,kHz mono): 1,806 natural field
recordings and 695 synthetic clips, each synthetic clip with its clean reference, from 28 states, 141
state/district pairs and 27 languages, with 5,157 labelled events in seven top-level categories (human non-speech
1,924; vehicle/traffic 1,281; animal 1,142; baby/child 496; phone/signal/alarm 188; singing/music 68;
appliance/machine 58). The test set holds 5,517 unlabelled clips; no test labels were released or accessed.

\textbf{External data (permitted by the rules~\cite{comp}, which allow additional public or proprietary data).}
We used the Gold tier of the Vaani noise-event dataset~\cite{vaanids,vaani} (10,772 clips, 19,414
verified-timestamp events; CC\,BY\,4.0, gated access which we hold) for every member except wsA and wlP, and its
Silver tier (unverified timestamps) for two members, wlHS (14,529 clips extracted from the Hugging Face release)
and bFS (the first 12,000 clips of the Kaggle copy ayanokoji2028/vaani-noise-hf of~\cite{vaanids}, HF file
names), at
loss weight 0.4 (member tags are defined in Sec.~\ref{sec:sed}). Overlap with the competition data was checked by
Vaani file stem, a unique clip id:
Gold shares no clip with the test set (0 of 5,517) or the validation set (0 of 2,501) and no recording session
(file-name prefix) with any test clip; 13 Gold clips share a recording session with a validation clip. Both Silver
extractions exclude every validation and test file name by construction. Speaker/district overlap with the test
set was not checked (domain, not label, leakage).

\textbf{Test-side information.} Two uses are disclosed. (a)~The leaderboard score of each upload was the final
selection criterion (Sec.~\ref{sec:res}); Codabench~\cite{comp} lists 47 uploads by our team (46 scored; one,
995069 on 17 Oct after the final, failed and was not scored) and our first upload (921278, 10 Sep) is no longer
listed, so at least 48 were made, under a quota of 5 per day and 100 in total. (b)~Member wlP was trained on
pseudo-labels~\cite{lee} that three validation-only Whisper heads (small, medium and large, weighted 1/1/2)
produced on the 3,353 test clips without a library hit, a transductive use of unlabelled test audio; the
organisers list semi-supervised use of unlabelled Vaani audio as a novelty direction, which we read as covering
these clips. The fold-$f$ student only sees labels from teachers that never saw fold $f$ (leak-free OOF);
Table~\ref{tab:comp} reports the ensemble without it.

\textbf{Template library.} Outside the pasted spans ($\pm$150\,ms) the residual (mixture minus clean reference) of
a synthetic validation clip is zero at most samples (per-clip median 0, overall maximum $4.4\!\times\!10^{-3}$ at
full scale 1), so every labelled synthetic event cut from the residual is the pasted noise sample. Near-duplicates (NCC\,$>$\,0.995) were merged, leaving 1,198 templates, each
with its source clips for leave-one-clip-out (LOO) evaluation.

\textbf{Features.} Every detector works on a 20\,ms frame grid. For each encoder, the tokens of three or four
selected layers are averaged over frequency (AST, BEATs, Dasheng), layer-normalised per frame, averaged over layers
and interpolated from their centres onto the grid; Whisper's 20\,ms encoder frames are used directly, at the true
clip length. Targets are eight binary channels per frame (any noise plus the seven categories): 24\,\% of natural
reference events overlap in time with an event of another category, which the event-F1 term scores separately.

\textbf{Validation protocol.} All model selection used 5-fold cross-validation grouped by state/district
(GroupKFold~\cite{sklearn}), to mimic a test set from unseen districts; every validation number below is
out-of-fold (OOF) with the same decoding as the test run, the library in LOO mode and the null levels
cross-fitted (two halves of the natural clips calibrate each other; synthetic clips use all natural clips).
Fusion weights and decode parameters were fitted on these same OOF predictions (no nested CV), so the OOF scores
are optimistic for those hyperparameters.

\section{Proposed Method}
\textbf{Background.} Frame-level CRNN classifiers~\cite{cakir} are a standard approach to SED; Whisper's encoder
keeps background-sound identity~\cite{whisperat}, and AudioSet~\cite{audioset} transformers (AST~\cite{ast},
BEATs~\cite{beats}, Dasheng~\cite{dasheng}) are strong general audio encoders. The synthetic half is instead
detection of a known signal, for which the matched filter is SNR-optimal in white Gaussian noise~\cite{turin}; we
use its normalised form, fast NCC~\cite{lewis}.

\textbf{Matched filter.}\label{sec:tpl} For a test clip $x$ and template $T_i$, the matcher computes the NCC
$c_i(s)$ at every sample lag $s$ with FFTs on the GPU. A per-template null level $\nu_i$ is the 0.999 quantile of
$\max_s c_i(s)$ over the natural validation clips. Candidates are kept when the margin $c_i(s)-\nu_i\ge 0.1$ and
selected greedily by margin with at most 30\,\% mutual overlap. In LOO mode on validation the matcher alone scores
F1 0.9819 / Dice 0.9723 on the 695 synthetic clips (Table~\ref{tab:comp}); it fires on 687 of them and on 73 of
the 1,806 natural clips.

\textbf{SED ensemble.}\label{sec:sed} Each member is a BiGRU (hidden 192, dropout 0.3) with a linear layer to eight
channels, trained with binary cross-entropy (masked to the clip's frames, per-channel weights as in the released
code) and SpecAugment-style feature masking~\cite{specaug} on random crops, AdamW~\cite{adamw} (weight decay $10^{-2}$) under a one-cycle schedule
peaking at $10^{-3}$; the weights after the last epoch are kept. Nine members keep the encoder frozen; each is the
mean of its five fold heads: wsA (whisper-small, layers 3/6/9/12, validation only, 25 epochs, batch 16, 600-frame
crops); wlP (whisper-large-v3, layers 8/16/24/32, the wsA recipe with pseudo-labels at weight 0.5); wlH, wlH2, wlH3
(large-v3, validation + Gold, 8 epochs, batch 8, 400-frame crops, seeds 0/1/2); wlHS (as wlH with Silver, 6
epochs); aH, bH, dH (AST / BEATs / Dasheng, layers 4/8/12, the wlH recipe). Three members update the encoder: aFG
(all 12 AST blocks, encoder learning rate $2\!\times\!10^{-5}$, head $10^{-3}$, 3 epochs, batch 8, fp16 autocast,
gradient checkpointing, validation + Gold, 5 folds), bFG (the same on BEATs with the patch embedding frozen) and
bFS (BEATs fine-tuned on all 2,501 validation clips + Gold + Silver, 2 epochs, batch 8, 6,320 steps, no hold-out).
The ensemble probability is the weighted mean
\begin{equation}
p = \Big(\sum\nolimits_m w_m p_m\Big)\Big/\sum\nolimits_m w_m ,
\end{equation}
with weights wsA 0.5, wlP 3, wlH 2, wlH2 6, wlH3 1, wlHS 6, aH 8, aFG 8, bH 8, bFG 8, dH 4, bFS 4. The weights of
the eleven members with OOF predictions were set by coordinate descent over a coarse grid on the natural clips
(\texttt{tune\_weights.py}; the matcher dominates the synthetic ones); bFS's weight was a leaderboard probe. Members
were added one at a time; our working rule was that a candidate had to raise the pooled OOF score with at least
four of five district folds up before it was given one upload (``one-slot probe''), which decided; the per-member
OOF records were not retained, so this is stated as practice, not demonstrated.

\textbf{Decoding and fusion.} Natural clips use two branches: $p_h=\min(p_{\text{hns}},p_0)$ for human non-speech
(hns) and $p_o=\min(\max_{c\ne\text{hns}}p_c,p_0)$ for the other categories, $p_0$ being the any-noise channel.
Each branch is decoded peak-relatively: a region is detected where the median-filtered curve (window 15 / 7
frames) clears a floor of 0.2, is kept if its peak reaches 0.34 / 0.30, and both boundaries are placed where the
curve crosses $\alpha$ times that event's own peak ($\alpha=0.6$ / 0.5). This replaces one global threshold; when
introduced (before upload 924220) it was worth +0.029 natural OOF event F1 on the same probabilities. Events
shorter than 0.1\,s are dropped, gaps below 0.1\,s / 2.0\,s are closed, and the branches are merged with duplicate
removal at IoU 0.5. A clip with any library hit is treated as synthetic: its events are the template events plus
any SED event (any-noise channel, threshold 0.7, median 3, minimum 0.1\,s, gap 0.04\,s) that does not overlap them
(all parameters: \texttt{models/fusion\_Q1\_bFSadd4.json}).

\textbf{Compute and software.} Python 3.13.6, PyTorch 2.14.0 (CUDA 12.6). Frozen heads (on cached float16
features), all aFG folds and bFG folds 0--2 were trained on one RTX 4060 Laptop GPU (8\,GB); bFG folds 3--4 and bFS
on Kaggle T4 GPUs. Test inference: five frozen-backbone and eleven fine-tuned encoder passes (5 aFG,
5 bFG, bFS) over the 5,517 clips, and 84.6\,min for the matched filter on the laptop GPU (FFT up to $2^{19}$).
Pretrained encoders: openai/whisper-small and
whisper-large-v3~\cite{whisper} (Apache\,2.0), MIT/ast-finetuned-audioset-10-10-0.4593~\cite{ast} (BSD\,3), BEATs
iter3+ AS2M~\cite{beats} (MIT), mispeech/dasheng-base~\cite{dasheng} (Apache\,2.0); all public. The original
encoders are not redistributed; the eleven fine-tuned AST/BEATs checkpoints are released as derivatives under
BSD\,3 / MIT with the original notices.

\textbf{Leaderboard.} Table~\ref{tab:res} traces the system from the first upload (1.4591) to the final 1.6009,
the highest of all our uploads listed on Codabench. The changes between the first upload and v14 (more members,
Gold data, the split and peak-relative decodes) are not separable in our records and account for most of the gain
(to 1.5808); fine-tuning AST, dropping aA and adding BEATs and Dasheng members add +0.0131; decoding changes
tuned on OOF predictions add +0.0063; bFS adds +0.0007, below what we can distinguish from noise. The gap to
rank~1 (1.6173) is 0.0164: event F1 0.0092, Dice 0.0072. Selection was leaderboard-guided throughout, so the
final score carries an optimistic bias that we did not quantify. OOF and leaderboard scores of one configuration
differ by 0.12 (1.4787 vs 1.6002); we attribute this to the test set being 39\,\% synthetic-like (2,164 of 5,517
clips have a library hit, against 28\,\% truly synthetic and 30\,\% with a LOO hit in validation) and to the test
run using the full library.

\textbf{Components.} Table~\ref{tab:comp} separates the two branches: on the synthetic clips the matcher alone
reaches 1.9542 and the SED alone 1.3378; on the natural clips the SED alone gives 1.1409, the matcher alone 0.0691,
and the fusion adds +0.016 (template events in the 73 natural clips with a hit). wlP is worth +0.0028 overall. The
natural clips remain the bottleneck (F1 0.45 against 0.99 synthetic): on a pre-v14 ensemble's natural OOF
predictions only 12.9\,\% of reference events were truly missed while 52\,\% overlapped a prediction that failed a
boundary tolerance, hence the focus on per-event boundary placement. Also tried and absent from the final system: a soft-Dice loss term (wlHSD, refuted out-of-fold), a stacker
over the members, CLAP and CED~\cite{ced} members, a fine-tuned Whisper (wFG), a fine-tuned Dasheng (dFG) and a
second AST seed; their configuration files (\texttt{fusion\_STK*}, \texttt{K2/K3}, \texttt{Q1\_eH4}, \texttt{W*},
\texttt{G\_*}, \texttt{D\_*}) are in the repository; the basis of each rejection was not uniformly recorded.

\textbf{Reproduction.} After the final upload the workspace lost the backbone weights, the fine-tuned checkpoints,
all cached test probabilities and the test template candidates; the 45 frozen fold heads, the configurations,
the template library and the scored zip survived. We retrained aFG, bFG and bFS with the identical scripts and
default seeds on Kaggle T4 GPUs (the originals of aFG and bFG folds 0--2 were trained on the laptop GPU), re-ran
the matcher, and recomputed the frozen members from the released heads and re-downloaded public encoders. The
retrained aFG/bFG give 1.4771 OOF against 1.4787 with the originals (Table~\ref{tab:comp}); the rebuilt submission
flags the same 2,164 clips as synthetic-like, is identical on 41.8\,\% of the clips (2,008 of 2,164 synthetic-like,
298 of 3,353 natural-like; 13,442 vs 13,451 events) and scores 1.9858 (F1 0.9943 / Dice 0.9915) under the
competition metric with the scored file as reference. The released
fine-tuned checkpoints (eleven files, 3.96\,GB, release assets linked from the repository) are thus not
bit-identical to the scored ones (different GPU, nondeterministic kernels); bFS has no OOF estimate, so its
equivalence rests on that agreement. Frozen members and matcher are deterministic up to floating-point differences
of the encoder and FFT passes. The pseudo-label file and teacher heads of wlP are not released.

\textbf{Limitations.} The matched filter relies on the test set reusing the validation noise library; with fresh
waveforms the system falls back to the SED branch (1.2005 OOF). The ensemble (five frozen backbones, eleven
fine-tuned encoders) was chosen for score, not cost; wlP uses test audio transductively; natural-clip event F1
(0.45 OOF) is the weakest part.

\textbf{Conclusion.} Known-signal detection on the synthetic half and frame-level SED with per-event boundary
placement on the natural half gave a 5th-place system, released with heads, configurations and both zips.
