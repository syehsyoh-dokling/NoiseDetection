\title{Matched-Filter Noise Recovery and a Twelve-Member Sound-Event Ensemble for Indic Noise Event Detection}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Saifuddin}
\IEEEauthorblockA{Team syehsyoh; Senior Digital Transformation (AI \& Automation) Expert; Datathon@IndoML 2026,
Track 1\\ Code, heads and submissions: \url{https://github.com/syehsyoh-dokling/NoiseDetection}}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Track~1 asks for the onset and offset of every noise event in Vaani field recordings, scored by an event-based F1
(both boundaries within 20\,\% of the reference duration, at least 50\,ms) plus a per-clip segment Dice. Our final
submission (Codabench submission 994586) placed 5th of 82 teams with a combined score of 1.600895 (F1 0.780264,
Dice 0.820631; maximum 2.0). The synthetic clips appear to be clean speech with noise pasted from a finite
library; mixture minus the released clean reference recovers the pasted waveforms, and a normalised
cross-correlation (NCC) matched filter over 1,198 such templates locates them in the test clips. Natural clips go
to a frame-level sound-event detector (SED): a weighted mean of twelve members (fold-averaged bidirectional-GRU
heads) over frozen or fine-tuned Whisper, AST, BEATs and Dasheng encoders, with per-event peak-relative boundaries. Training
uses the public Vaani Gold/Silver tiers and, for one member, pseudo-labels on unlabelled test audio. We report the
lineage, out-of-fold component scores, compute cost, and the reproduction after the fine-tuned checkpoints were
lost.
\end{abstract}

\section{Training Data and Preprocessing}
\textbf{Vaani data.} The released validation set holds 2,501 clips (3.51\,h, 16\,kHz mono): 1,806 natural field
recordings and 695 synthetic clips, each synthetic clip with its clean reference, from 28 states, 141
state/district pairs and 27 languages, with 5,157 labelled events in seven top-level categories (human non-speech
1,924; vehicle/traffic 1,281; animal 1,142; baby/child 496; phone/signal/alarm 188; singing/music 68;
appliance/machine 58). The test set holds 5,517 unlabelled clips; no test labels were released or accessed.

\textbf{External data (permitted by the rules~\cite{comp}, which allow additional public or proprietary data).}
We used the Gold tier of the Vaani noise-event dataset~\cite{vaanids,vaani} (10,772 clips, 19,414
verified-timestamp events; CC\,BY\,4.0, gated access which we hold) for every member except wsA and wlP, and its
Silver tier (unverified timestamps) for wlHS (14,529 clips from the Hugging Face release) and bFS (the first
12,000 clips of the Kaggle copy ayanokoji2028/vaani-noise-hf of~\cite{vaanids}, HF file names), at loss weight
0.4 (member tags: Table~\ref{tab:members}). Overlap was checked by Vaani file stem, a unique clip id: Gold shares
no clip with the test set (0 of 5,517) or the validation set (0 of 2,501) and no recording session (file-name
prefix) with any test clip; 13 of 10,772 Gold clips share a session with a validation clip (negligible for OOF).
Silver extractions exclude validation and test file names (by name, not session). Speaker/district overlap with
the test set was not checked (domain, not label, leakage).

\textbf{Test-side information.} (a)~The leaderboard score of each upload was the final selection criterion;
Codabench~\cite{comp} lists 47 of our uploads (46 scored; 995069, 17 Oct, after the final scored upload, failed) and the first
(921278, 10 Sep) is no longer listed, so at least 48 were made (quota 5/day, 100 total). (b)~Member wlP was
trained on pseudo-labels~\cite{lee} that three validation-only Whisper heads (small, medium, large, weighted
1/1/2) produced on the 3,353 test clips without a library hit: transductive use of unlabelled test audio (the
organisers list semi-supervised use of unlabelled Vaani audio as a novelty direction, not test clips
specifically). The fold-$f$ student only sees labels from teachers that never saw fold $f$ (leak-free OOF).

\textbf{Template library.} Outside the pasted spans ($\pm$150\,ms) the residual (mixture minus clean reference) of
a synthetic validation clip is zero at most samples (median of per-clip medians 0, maximum $4.4\!\times\!10^{-3}$
at full scale 1), so each labelled synthetic event cut from the residual is the pasted noise up to that residual. Near-duplicates
(NCC\,$>$\,0.995) were merged, leaving 1,198 templates, each with its source clips for leave-one-clip-out (LOO)
evaluation.

\textbf{Features.} On a 20\,ms grid, the tokens of three or four layers per encoder are averaged over frequency
(AST, BEATs, Dasheng), layer-normalised per frame, averaged over layers and interpolated onto the grid (Whisper
frames are used directly). Targets are eight binary channels (any noise + seven categories): 24\,\% of natural
reference events overlap in time with an event of another category, which the event-F1 term scores separately.

\textbf{Validation protocol.} Model selection used 5-fold cross-validation grouped by state/district (GroupKFold;
500--501 clips, 27--29 districts per fold) to mimic unseen test districts; every validation number is out-of-fold
(OOF) with the test decoding, the library in LOO mode and cross-fitted null levels (two halves of the natural
clips calibrate each other). Fusion weights, decode and matcher parameters were fitted on these OOF predictions
(no nested CV), so OOF scores are optimistic for them.

\section{Proposed Method}
\textbf{Background.} CRNN frame classifiers~\cite{cakir,mesaros} are a standard approach to SED, scored with event-
and segment-based metrics~\cite{sedmetrics}; semi-supervised SED is a DCASE task~\cite{dcase}. Whisper keeps
background-sound identity~\cite{whisperat}; AudioSet~\cite{audioset} transformers (AST~\cite{ast},
BEATs~\cite{beats}, Dasheng~\cite{dasheng}) are strong audio encoders. The synthetic half is known-signal detection,
as in fingerprinting~\cite{wang}, where the matched filter is SNR-optimal in white Gaussian noise~\cite{turin}; we
use a normalised form computed with FFTs~\cite{lewis}.

\textbf{Matched filter.}\label{sec:tpl} For a test clip $x$ and template $T_i$, the matcher computes the
energy-normalised cross-correlation (cosine, no mean removal) $c_i(s)$ at every sample lag $s$ on the GPU and keeps the top 2 peaks per template and clip (0.3\,s
apart, NCC $>$ 0.2). A per-template null level $\nu_i$ is the 0.999 quantile of $\max_s c_i(s)$ over the natural
validation clips (unstored values count as 0.2, so the effective NCC threshold is $\ge$\,0.3). Candidates are kept
when the margin $c_i(s)-\nu_i\ge 0.1$ and selected greedily by margin with at most 30\,\% mutual overlap. In LOO mode the matcher alone scores F1 0.9819 /
Dice 0.9723 on the 695 synthetic clips (Table~\ref{tab:comp}); it fires on 687 of them and on 73 of the 1,806
natural clips.

\textbf{SED ensemble.}\label{sec:sed} Each head is a BiGRU (hidden 192, dropout 0.3) with a linear layer to the 8
channels, trained with binary cross-entropy (masked to the clip's frames, per-channel weights), SpecAugment-style
masking~\cite{specaug} and AdamW~\cite{adamw} (wd $10^{-2}$) under a one-cycle schedule (peak $10^{-3}$; ft
encoders $2\!\times\!10^{-5}$ with fp16 autocast and gradient checkpointing), last epoch kept; a member's test
probability is the mean of its 5 fold heads (bFS: one model). Table~\ref{tab:members} lists the members; the
ensemble is $p=\sum_m w_m p_m/\sum_m w_m$. The weights of the eleven members with OOF predictions were set by coordinate descent over a coarse grid on the
natural clips (\texttt{tune\_weights.py}; the matcher dominates the synthetic ones); bFS's weight was a leaderboard
probe. Members were added one or two at a time, each step given one leaderboard upload (``one-slot probe'') whose score then
decided; per-member OOF records were not retained.

\textbf{Decoding and fusion.} Natural clips use two branches: $p_h=\min(p_{\text{hns}},p_0)$ for human non-speech
(hns) and $p_o=\min(\max_{c\ne\text{hns}}p_c,p_0)$ for the other categories, $p_0$ being the any-noise channel.
Each branch is decoded peak-relatively: a region is detected where the median-filtered curve (window 15 / 7
frames) clears a floor of 0.2, is kept if its peak reaches 0.34 / 0.30, and both boundaries are placed where the
curve crosses $\alpha$ times that event's own peak ($\alpha=0.6$ / 0.5), +0.029 natural OOF F1 over one global
threshold when introduced (before v14). Events
shorter than 0.1\,s are dropped, gaps below 0.1\,s / 2.0\,s are closed, and the branches are merged with duplicate
removal at IoU 0.5. A clip with any library hit is treated as synthetic: its events are the template events plus
any SED event (any-noise channel, threshold 0.7, median 3, minimum 0.1\,s, gap 0.04\,s) that does not overlap
them (\texttt{models/fusion\_Q1\_bFSadd4.json}). The system is offline and non-causal: the heads are
bidirectional, the decoder needs the whole event, the encoders see the whole clip.

\textbf{Compute and software.} Python 3.13.6, PyTorch 2.14.0 (CUDA 12.6). Frozen heads (on cached float16
features), all aFG folds and bFG folds 0--2 were trained on one RTX 4060 Laptop GPU (8\,GB); bFG folds 3--4 and bFS
on Kaggle T4 GPUs (default GPU image, Python 3.13). On one T4 per job (retrain logs): one aFG fold 64--67\,min, one bFG fold about 55\,min, bFS
75\,min; one fine-tuned encoder pass over the 5,517 test clips (10.81\,h of audio) 4.5--5\,min (an estimated
55\,min for the eleven); the five frozen-backbone passes plus downloads under 1.4\,h; the matched filter
84.6\,min on the laptop GPU (FFT up to $2^{19}$). Public encoders (IDs in the README; not redistributed,
fine-tuned derivatives keep the notices): Whisper~\cite{whisper}, Dasheng (Apache\,2.0), AST (BSD\,3), BEATs (MIT).

\textbf{Leaderboard.} Table~\ref{tab:res}: from 1.4591 to the final 1.6009 (our highest listed upload). The
changes up to v14 (more members, Gold data, split and peak-relative decodes) are not separable in our records and
give most of the gain; fine-tuning AST, dropping aA and adding BEATs and Dasheng add +0.0131; decoding changes
(OOF-motivated, LB-confirmed) +0.0063; bFS +0.0007, not established. The gap to rank~1 (1.6173) is 0.0164 (F1
0.0092, Dice 0.0072). Leaderboard-guided selection leaves an unquantified optimistic bias. OOF and LB differ by
0.12 (1.4787 vs 1.6002); the test set is 39\,\% synthetic-like (2,164 of 5,517 clips with a library hit; 28\,\%
synthetic, 30\,\% LOO hits in validation), and re-weighting the per-clip OOF Dice by that mix gives
$0.608\cdot0.7105+0.392\cdot0.9943=0.822$ vs LB 0.8195, explaining the Dice part (F1 needs test event counts).

\textbf{Components and errors.} Table~\ref{tab:comp}: on synthetic clips the matcher alone gives 1.9542, the SED
alone 1.3378; on natural clips the SED alone 1.1409, the matcher alone 0.0691, the fusion +0.016 over SED. In the
73 natural clips with a hit, 64 of the 77 template events (83\,\%) match a reference event, and these clips score
1.3047 fused vs 0.9519 with the SED decode. Leaving one member out lowers pooled OOF by 0.0097 (bFG), 0.0069
(dH), 0.0061 (bH) and 0.0027--0.0035 (aFG, wlH, wlP, wlH2, wlHS), but raises it by 0.0001--0.0014 for wsA, wlH3
and aH, which thus carry no OOF support (weights unchanged since v14). wlP's paired fold difference is positive on
4/5 folds (mean +0.0026, $t\approx1.7$, not significant); its leaderboard effect was not measured.
Table~\ref{tab:cat} (recall only): 62\,\% of human non-speech events (median 0.32\,s) are recovered but only
21.5--30\,\% of the other categories; recall does not fall with duration (baby, 0.70\,s, is lowest), and the
other-branch decode (2.0\,s gap closing merges nearby events), boundary ambiguity and scarcity are candidate causes
we did not separate (on a pre-v14 ensemble 12.9\,\% of natural events were truly missed, 52\,\% failed a boundary
tolerance). Tried and rejected: a soft-Dice loss term (wlHSD,
refuted out-of-fold), a stacker, CLAP and CED~\cite{ced} members, a fine-tuned Whisper (wFG), a fine-tuned
Dasheng (dFG) and a second AST seed (configurations in \texttt{models/fusion\_*.json}; reasons not uniformly
recorded).

\textbf{Reproduction.} After the final upload we lost backbones, fine-tuned checkpoints, cached test probabilities
and template candidates; frozen heads, configurations, library and scored zip survived. We retrained aFG, bFG
and bFS with the same scripts and seeds on Kaggle T4s, re-ran the matcher and recomputed the frozen members.
The retrained aFG/bFG give 1.4771 OOF (vs 1.4787); the rebuilt submission flags the identical set of 2,164
synthetic-like clips, is identical on 41.8\,\% of clips (2,008 of 2,164 synthetic-like, 298 of 3,353 natural-like;
13,442 vs 13,451 events) and scores 1.9858 (F1 0.9943 / Dice 0.9915) with the scored file as reference. The
rebuilt SED probabilities are not bit-identical (three members retrained; different GPUs and software stack; causes
not separated); the released fine-tuned checkpoints (eleven,
3.96\,GB, release assets) are the retrained ones, and bFS (no OOF) rests on this agreement. wlP's pseudo-labels and
teacher heads are not released: its five heads make inference, not retraining, reproducible.

\textbf{Limitations and conclusion.} The matcher needs the test set to reuse the validation library (else the SED,
1.2005 OOF, carries the score); the ensemble is large, offline and non-causal; wlP is transductive; natural F1
(0.45) is the weakest part. The resulting 5th-place system is released: code, configurations, all member heads
and retrained checkpoints (wlP's pseudo-labels and two teacher heads excepted).
